# The Hilltop Defense (8 Months Ago)

**Characters:** [[Cael Stormcrow|Caelum "Cael" Stormcrow]] & [[Kane|Kaelan "Kane" Vyreth]]
**Location:** [[Hilltop]], a farming settlement east of [[Daggerford]]
**Threat:** Gnoll war band (~100 strong)
**Organizations:** [[The Ashen Spears]], [[Emerald Enclave]]

---

## The Warning

Cael was three days into a solo patrol for the [[Emerald Enclave]] - checking on reports of unnatural predator behavior in the foothills east of [[Daggerford]] - when he tasted the wrongness on the wind. Not corruption this time. Something simpler. Blood and smoke, carried on air that should have been clean.

He climbed to high ground and saw them: a gnoll war band, a hundred strong at least, moving through the lowlands toward Hilltop. A farming settlement of maybe a hundred and fifty souls, walls meant for keeping out wolves, not demon-touched hyena-folk in a killing frenzy.

No time to run for help. No time to warn them conventionally.

Cael found the old beacon tower on the ridge above the settlement - cold, unlit, its signal fire meant to summon aid from Liam's Hold. He didn't bother with flint and tinder.

He raised The Fulcrum toward a clear blue sky and _pulled_.

The lightning bolt that struck the beacon was visible for twenty miles. The thunder rolled across the hills like a war drum. The fire that erupted was bright enough to see in daylight - the old signal, the call for aid, burning against the afternoon sky.

The gnolls heard it too. They started running.

---

## The Response

At Liam's Hold, [[Kane]] and [[The Ashen Spears]] were between assignments - a rare few days of rest before their next [[Lords' Alliance]] contract. They saw the beacon flare from the common room window.

Dorvek, the grizzled dwarf who served as Kane's second, was already on his feet. "That's Hilltop's beacon."

Mira, their half-elf scout, squinted at the clear sky. "Lightning strike? There's no storm."

Kane's hand tightened on his glaive, shadows instinctively curling around his fingers. He'd heard stories about the [[Emerald Enclave]]'s "walking storm" - the white-haired man who'd stood in the [[Delimbiyr River]] and called lightning for six hours. If _he_ was lighting warning beacons, something bad was coming.

"Gear up," Kane said. "All of us. Now."

The Ashen Spears rode hard.

## The Stand

By the time Kane's group crested the ridge above Hilltop, the situation was clear: the town's gates were closed, gnolls were circling the walls testing for weakness, and one man in strange copper-laced armor stood on the eastern watchtower, lightning arcing from his hands to pick off scouts.

Kane's shadow-touched eyes took in the tactical situation with the cold precision of a man who'd spent six years surviving as a war-slave. The eastern road was the obvious assault point - narrow approach, barely wide enough for three gnolls abreast. Perfect chokepoint.

The white-haired man on the tower saw them coming. He raised a hand - not waving, just acknowledging - then pointed toward the eastern approach and mimed a wall with his hands.

Kane understood. _You hold the gap. I'll break them before they reach you._

"Dorvek, you're with me at the gate. Spear wall, standard formation." He gestured to the others. "Flanks - watch for climbers. Reserve and support where needed."

The Ashen Spears moved into position like water finding its level. No wasted motion. No questions.

Kane took center position in the gap, his glaive creating a ten-foot radius of lethality that nothing would cross without his permission. Dorvek anchored his left. Two warriors in a doorway, the rest of the Spears filling the gaps. It would have to be enough.

On the tower above, the storm-touched man spread his arms wide, and the sky began to darken.

## The Battle

The gnolls came as the sun touched the horizon - not a probing attack this time, but a full assault. Fifty warriors in the first wave, howling their war-cries, crude weapons raised.

Cael hit them first.

He exhaled, and his breath became weather.

Twenty feet out from the gate, reality shifted. A sphere of compressed storm manifested in midair - wind screaming, rain lashing sideways, lightning crackling through it constantly. The first gnolls to hit it staggered, battered by winds strong enough to knock them off their feet. Those who pushed through found themselves wading against a hurricane, slowed to a crawl.

And then the lightning started.

Cael pointed from the tower, casual as indicating a target on a map. The sphere responded - a bolt of electricity lanced out and punched through a gnoll's chest. He pointed again. Another bolt. Again. The sphere crackled and obeyed, striking with lethal precision every few seconds while the wind inside continued to batter anything that entered.

The gnolls that made it through arrived at the gate scattered, wounded, disoriented.

Kane was waiting.

His glaive moved with mechanical precision. Ten feet of reach meant the gnolls couldn't close; they died trying. One lunged past the spear-point and Kane's blade snapped across, hamstringing it mid-stride. It collapsed, and Dorvek's spear finished it.

"ROTATE!"

The Spears shifted like a well-oiled machine - fresh warrior to the point, formation never breaking.

The sphere held for nearly a minute, chewing through the first wave, before Cael let it collapse. Thirty gnolls dead or dying in the approach. Another dozen at the gate itself.

The survivors pulled back, regrouping in the treeline. Kane allowed himself a breath.

First wave down. But that was less than half their numbers.

---

## The Breach

They came again at full dark, smarter this time. Three groups hit simultaneously - the main force at the eastern gate, a flanking group at the north wall with grappling hooks, and a third probing the western approach.

Cael split his attention, throwing lightning where he could, but he couldn't be everywhere. When he dropped another sphere of howling wind at the eastern approach to slow the main assault, the northern group made it over the wall.

"BREACH NORTH!" Mira's voice cut through the chaos.

Eight gnolls in the street behind the main defense. Militia engaging - farmers with spears, brave but outmatched. One man was already down, another backing up with his weapon shaking.

Kane made the call in a heartbeat. "Dorvek - hold the gate. I've got it."

He moved before anyone could argue.

Kane's form dissolved into writhing shadows. The temperature dropped. For a heartbeat, black ravens exploded outward - then he was _there_, between the gnolls and the fallen militiaman, glaive already moving.

The first gnoll died before it registered his arrival - glaive through the throat, clinical and precise. The second swung a crude axe at his head; Kane pivoted, let it pass, and the return stroke opened its belly.

The third tried to run.

"_Stop._"

Kane's voice carried unnatural weight. Shadows pooled across the cobblestones, spreading toward the fleeing gnoll like living things. Its legs locked mid-stride, muscles frozen by something deeper than fear.

The fourth gnoll charged him from behind. Kane didn't turn - just snapped his glaive backward in a reversed grip. The blade caught it across the thigh, and when it stumbled, trying to recover, the stored resonance in the wound _detonated_ - a crack of thunder that dropped the gnoll with its legs useless.

Three more behind it. Kane moved like death given purpose - strike, pivot, strike. His glaive's reach meant they couldn't close without dying. One got inside his guard; he dissolved into shadows and reformed three feet away, blade already descending.

Eight seconds. Eight gnolls down.

He walked to the frozen one, the last still breathing. His eyes had gone black, shadows curling around the glaive's blade like dark fire.

"You picked the wrong town."

The strike was less combat than execution.

Above, Cael had seen the breach. He'd also seen Kane handle it - all of it - in the time it took to draw three breaths.

_Hell of a thing_, he thought, lightning crackling between his fingers. _Good to know._

He turned his attention back to the main assault and called down thunder.

---

## The Breaking Point

The final push came an hour before dawn. The gnolls massed everything they had left - forty warriors, maybe more - for one concentrated assault on the eastern gate.

The spear wall held, but barely. The weight of numbers was pushing them back, boots sliding on blood-slick stone. Kane had rejoined the line, his glaive reaping through anything that got close, but there were too many.

"CAEL!" Kane's voice cut through the chaos. "FORMATION - NOW!"

Cael understood. He'd been holding back all night, saving the full weight of the storm for the right moment.

This was the moment.

He raised both hands toward the gnoll formation, shaped the lightning into a line, and _remembered_ - the mast, the fall, the moment he became something else. Salt crystallized in his beard. His scars blazed white. The Fulcrum hummed in his grip.

Then he stopped holding back.

The lightning bolt didn't strike so much as _erase_ - a hundred-foot line of white-hot fury that carved through the gnoll formation like a blade through smoke. The thunder hit a heartbeat later, a physical force that knocked warriors off their feet thirty yards from the impact. When it cleared, a smoking trench ran through the assault force, glass forming where sand had been, bodies scattered on either side.

The survivors broke. Not a tactical retreat - pure animal terror, scattering into the darkness.

Kane watched them go. His glaive dripped gnoll blood. Shadows still curled around his fingers.

"Don't pursue," he said quietly. "Let them carry the story."

---

## After

Dawn found them sitting on the wall near the eastern gate, watching the sun rise over a field of gnoll corpses.

Cael's Storm Cage armor still sparked occasionally, small pops of electricity discharging into the air. Kane sat beside him, shadows fading as the light grew, his glaive across his knees.

For a long moment, neither spoke.

"That teleport thing," Cael finally said. "Shadows and ravens. That's not subtle."

"More subtle than calling lightning from a clear sky." The corner of Kane's mouth twitched. Almost a wink.

"Fair." Cael's mouth quirked. "You moved fast. That breach - I was about to reposition, then you just... handled it."

"It's what I do." Kane studied him - the white hair, the strange armor, the eyes that shifted like storm-water. "That last bolt. You weren't casting. You were _channeling_."

Cael glanced at him, surprised. Most people didn't see the difference. "Something like that. Five years ago I was just a sailor reading weather patterns from a crow's nest. Then lightning struck our ship, I fell into the ocean, and..." He shrugged, static crackling across his pauldrons. "Now I'm this."

"The Raven Queen found me in a [[Thay|Thayan]] slave pit," [[Kane]] said quietly. "Granted me power to fight back against the necromancers. I know what it means to be changed by forces that don't explain themselves."

Another silence, more comfortable than the first.

"Your people," Cael said. "The Spears. They move like one thing with eight bodies."

"We were thirty when they took us." Kane's voice was flat, but something moved beneath it. "We're the survivors."

Cael nodded slowly. He understood losing people. [[The Moonrunner]] had taught him that. "They're lucky to have you."

"We're lucky to have each other." Kane looked at him directly. "You fight like you're used to being the most dangerous thing on the field."

"Usually am." No boast, just fact.

Kane just nodded. Results spoke for themselves.

Cael laughed - short, surprised. "Yeah. That was... different. Having backup."

The reinforcements from Liam's Hold arrived an hour later. They found the heroes still on the wall, the Ashen Spears checking wounds and cleaning weapons, and a field of gnoll bodies that would take half a day to burn.

Dorvek looked up at Kane and the white-haired stranger sitting together in the dawn light. Shadows and static. Ravens and lightning.

"Storm and shadow," the dwarf muttered to Mira, shaking his head. "Hell of a combination."

The name stuck.

## What They Remember

### Cael's Impression of Kane

Professional. Controlled in a way that suggested practice rather than nature - someone who'd learned to keep himself leashed. The shadow magic was cold where Cael's power ran hot, but similarly dangerous. The moment at the breach stayed with him: Kane dissolving into ravens, reappearing mid-strike, putting down eight gnolls in seconds without wasted motion. The way he'd frozen one with a word and executed it without hesitation. The Ashen Spears moved like a unit that had bled together, and Kane commanded them without wasted words. Good people to have at your back.

### Kane's Impression of Cael

Dangerous in the same way a thunderstorm is dangerous - not malicious, just powerful and pointed somewhere. The spheres of contained tempest, the surgical lightning strikes, the final bolt that carved a trench through the assault - the man fought like artillery given human form. But it was the precision that impressed Kane most. Not wild destruction, but controlled devastation exactly where it would do the most good. Practical, direct, dark humor. The kind of person who'd hold a line until he died or the enemy did. The kind of person worth knowing.

### The Ashen Spears' Take

"Storm and shadow. The lightning guy breaks their lines, Kane breaks anyone who gets through. Wouldn't want to be on the other side of that."
