# The Dead Three Temple

**Type:** Location / Conspiracy
**Status:** Active — partially explored
**Introduced:** [[Session 04 - Good Timing]]

## Summary
A former shrine to Lathander (god of dawn) exists in the basement beneath [[Viggio Martel]]'s manor. It has been desecrated and converted to worship of the Dead Three. A ritual was performed here — completed before the party arrived. Two bodies were left on the altar. [[The Death Tyrant]] was present, overseeing or concluding the ritual.

## What We Know

### The Temple
- Accessed via a secret door that opened from the sitting room area (possibly triggered by Kane's demand for Viggio to show himself)
- **Former temple of Lathander** — the Morninglord, god of dawn and renewal
- Now covered in **Dead Three symbology** — Bane (tyranny), Bhaal (murder), Myrkul (death)
- Candles, altars, undead present

### The Ritual
- Two slain bodies on the altar — identities unknown
- [[The Death Tyrant]] was present and appeared to conclude the ritual just as the party arrived
- He took **[[The Disk]]** — red and black, yin-yang style, gold rim — possibly the ritual's product or its key component
- The missing right index fingers (from [[The Dead Men's Fingers]]) were stored directly above in a locked chest — likely ritual components

### The Antimagic Effect
- All magic ceased when the death tyrant was present
- Restored when he left
- Source unclear: innate ability, the disk, or some other mechanism

## The Dead Three — FR Context
The Dead Three are:
- **Bane** — god of tyranny, strife, and hatred (Zhentarim historically served Bane)
- **Bhaal** — god of murder and assassination
- **Myrkul** — god of death and the dead

The Zhentarim's historical connection to Bane is notable. The murders (right index fingers removed = Bhaal's domain of assassination?), the undead and ritual (Myrkul), and the political tyranny and manipulation (Bane) — all three may be represented in what's been happening in [[Daggerford]].

## The Lathander Connection
Lathander and the Dead Three are fundamentally opposed — dawn vs. death, renewal vs. decay. Converting one of his shrines is not merely practical; it's a theological statement.
